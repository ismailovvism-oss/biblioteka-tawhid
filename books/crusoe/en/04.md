<!-- s001 -->
**Chapter Four. The Rover**

<!-- s002 -->
We saw her a little after eight in the morning, hull down in the south-east, and for four hours she was simply a sail.

<!-- s003 -->
That is the part nobody puts in the stories. It is not a shout and a boarding. It is four hours of watching a triangle of white get slightly larger, and knowing, and having nothing whatever to do about it.

<!-- s004 -->
The master went up twice with the glass. The second time he came down he gave the order to make all sail, and after that no one on that deck pretended any more.

<!-- s005 -->
She was a rover out of Salé, and she was faster than us by about a third, and every man aboard could do that arithmetic without being told the numbers.

<!-- s006 -->
![For four hours she was simply a sail.](media/ch04-a.jpg)

<!-- s007 -->
I remember the last hour very clearly.

<!-- s008 -->
The carpenter sat down on the deck and took his shoes off and put them side by side, neatly, as though he were going to bed. I asked him why. He said a man swims better without them and that his father had drowned in his shoes.

<!-- s009 -->
The cook went below and came up with a knife I had never seen him use for cooking.

<!-- s010 -->
And I stood at the rail with my father's rope coiled on my arm. I had carried it everywhere since the hold and could not have said why. I thought about the milestone on the road out of Yarmouth.

<!-- s011 -->
Four days' walk. I could have been three years in York by then, in a bad temper, alive.

<!-- s012 -->
⁂

<!-- s013 -->
She came up on our quarter about two in the afternoon and put a shot across us, and when we did not strike, she put the next one into us.

<!-- s014 -->
I will not make a battle of it. It lasted less time than it takes to eat a meal.

<!-- s015 -->
They were sixty or seventy men and we were nineteen. They came over the rail in three places at once and they did it without shouting, which was somehow worse. Our mate went down in the first half minute. A man beside me put up his hands and was not hurt, and I saw that and put up mine.

<!-- s016 -->
I have wondered since whether I should be ashamed of how quickly I did it.

<!-- s017 -->
I have decided that I should not. There is a kind of courage that is only a slower way of getting men killed, and the mate had it, and he is at the bottom of the sea off the Canaries.

<!-- s018 -->
⁂

<!-- s019 -->
They took the ship, and they took us, and then they sorted us.

<!-- s020 -->
That is the word. Sorted. We stood on our own deck and a man walked down the line. He looked at us the way my father looked at a delivery of hemp: for what would be usable, and for what would have to be thrown out.

<!-- s021 -->
He took the carpenter, obviously. He took the two strongest hands.

<!-- s022 -->
He came to me and I was eighteen and soft-handed and no use to anybody, and I understood, standing there, that being of no use was the most dangerous thing I had ever been.

<!-- s023 -->
So I did the only thing I could think of.

<!-- s024 -->
I said, in the little Portuguese I had picked up on the Guinea coast, that I could write and keep accounts.

<!-- s025 -->
He looked at me for a while.

<!-- s026 -->
Then he said, in better Portuguese than mine, "Show me."

<!-- s027 -->
They brought up the ship's book. I sat down on a hatch cover on a captured deck with a dead man forty feet away and I ruled a line and set down a column of figures in my father's small square hand.

<!-- s028 -->
That is how I lived.

<!-- s029 -->
⁂

<!-- s030 -->
The rover's captain kept me for himself.

<!-- s031 -->
I learned later that this was luck of a kind that people spend their whole lives without. The men who were sorted the other way went to the galleys, and I never heard of any of them again, and I have never once let myself imagine it in detail.

<!-- s032 -->
We were nine days to Salé.

<!-- s033 -->
![That is the word. Sorted.](media/ch04-b.jpg)

<!-- s034 -->
The river came up brown and slow between two towns, and the whole place stood with its back to the land and its face to the sea.

<!-- s035 -->
There were ships from everywhere along the quays. Dutch cloth going ashore. Sugar in barrels that leaked sweetness onto the stones. A crate of ostrich feathers had broken somewhere and there were feathers caught along the base of a wall, moving in the wind.

<!-- s036 -->
I stood in that boat with my hands tied in front of me. I looked at it all and I thought, with perfect clarity: *this is a port like Hull is a port.*

<!-- s037 -->
Men were arguing about the price of something. A boy was selling water out of a skin. A dog was asleep in a doorway with its legs out.

<!-- s038 -->
I do not know what I had expected. Something more like the end of the world, I suppose.

<!-- s039 -->
That is the first thing Salé taught me, and it took about ninety seconds: the places where the worst thing in your life happens to you are ordinary places, and they go on being ordinary while it happens.

<!-- s040 -->
⁂

<!-- s041 -->
I was not sold in the market.

<!-- s042 -->
The captain kept me, which meant I went straight from the boat to a house. The house had a courtyard with a lemon tree in it. I slept the first night in a store-room off that courtyard, on a mat, with the door barred from the outside.

<!-- s043 -->
In the morning a woman I never learned the name of gave me bread and oil, and looked at me for a moment with an expression I could not read at all, and went away.

<!-- s044 -->
Then a man came and put a hoe in my hands and took me out to the garden, and that was that.

<!-- s045 -->
I was property for two years.

<!-- s046 -->
⁂

<!-- s047 -->
I want to set down exactly what it was like, because people ask, and because most of what they imagine is wrong in both directions.

<!-- s048 -->
Nobody beat me. Not once. I was worth money and money is not improved by damage.

<!-- s049 -->
I was fed the same food the household ate, which was better than anything I had eaten in London.

<!-- s050 -->
And none of that is the point. I did not understand that it was not the point until about the third month. I was pulling weeds in a hot garden, and I stopped, and looked at my hands, and thought: *if I were to die here this afternoon, the loss would be his.*

<!-- s051 -->
Not my mother's. Not my father's. His.

<!-- s052 -->
I had been sold out of my own life into somebody's inventory. I was a number in a ledger, and I knew exactly what that felt like from the other side, because I had written the ledgers.

<!-- s053 -->
That is the thing they took, and it is the only thing they took, and it is everything.

<!-- s054 -->
⁂

<!-- s055 -->
They could not say Robinson.

<!-- s056 -->
It came out of their mouths with the wrong bones in it. Within a week it had worn down to two syllables that were nearly right and not quite. After a month that was simply what I was called.

<!-- s057 -->
I answered to it. Of course I answered to it. You answer to whatever gets you fed.

<!-- s058 -->
But I lay on my mat that night and thought about my father, and about Bremen, and about a name that went into an English mouth as Kreutznaer and came out of it, thirty years later, as Crusoe.

<!-- s059 -->
York had filed my father's name.

<!-- s060 -->
Salé filed mine.

<!-- s061 -->
I remember thinking, with a kind of black satisfaction: *well, at least now I know what he lost.*

<!-- s062 -->
![This is a port like Hull is a port.](media/ch04-c.jpg)

<!-- s063 -->
The last thing I will say about that first month is about the sound.

<!-- s064 -->
It came before dawn, from the tower, and it went out over the flat roofs, and it was a man's voice, unaccompanied, holding notes longer than any voice at home would have held them.

<!-- s065 -->
I did not understand one word of it. For a long time I did not want to.

<!-- s066 -->
But it came five times a day, always, in every weather, and the whole city bent itself around it. The arguing stopped and started again. The shops shut and opened. The man in the next courtyard put down what he was carrying and went in.

<!-- s067 -->
After a few weeks I found that I no longer needed to guess the hour.

<!-- s068 -->
I would be in the garden with the hoe. It would begin. And I would think: *so it is that time.*

<!-- s069 -->
My father, on a cold roof in York, had told me that one day I might have to know where I was without being told.

<!-- s070 -->
He had meant the star. He had meant latitude and a hand held up against the sky.

<!-- s071 -->
It did not occur to me for a long time that the same trick works on time. I had begun to learn it in a garden I was not allowed to leave, from a voice I could not understand — saying words I would one day say myself.
