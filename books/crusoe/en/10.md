**Chapter Ten. The Print**

<!-- s002 -->
One print.

<!-- s003 -->
That is what I want you to understand before anything else. Not a trail. Not a line of them coming up out of the water and going away into the trees, which would have been a fact I could follow.

<!-- s004 -->
One naked human foot, pressed into wet sand, above the last tide line.

<!-- s005 -->
I stood over it for a long time.

<!-- s006 -->
Then I went twenty paces one way and twenty the other, and there was nothing. I went down to the water and back. I went up into the dry sand where nothing holds a mark. Nothing.

<!-- s007 -->
A man does not arrive at a beach, put down one foot, and vanish. Which meant the rest had been taken by the tide, and that meant it was recent, and that meant somebody had been standing exactly there, on my island, within about six hours.

<!-- s008 -->
![One naked human foot, pressed into wet sand.](media/ch10-a.jpg)

<!-- s009 -->
I do not remember getting home.

<!-- s010 -->
I have a clear memory of standing over the print, and then a clear memory of being inside my own door with the ladder pulled up after me and my heart going so hard I could feel it in my teeth, and almost nothing in between. I am told this happens to soldiers.

<!-- s011 -->
I stayed in for three days.

<!-- s012 -->
Three days, on no food to speak of, listening. I did not light a fire. I did not go out to the goats, and they suffered for it, and I lay in the dark hearing them and did not go.

<!-- s013 -->
I have been frightened perhaps six times in my life in a way that stopped me thinking. In the hold of that ship. Twice in the storm. On the bank of that river with the cat. And three days in my own house because of a hole in some sand.

<!-- s014 -->
⁂

<!-- s015 -->
On the fourth day I made myself go back, and I made myself do the one thing I had been avoiding, which was to measure it.

<!-- s016 -->
I had told myself a story by then. A good one. I had gone down to that beach some weeks before to look at the boat, and I had been barefoot, and it was my own print, and I was an old man on his own who had frightened himself with his own foot.

<!-- s017 -->
I lay down beside it and put my foot into it.

<!-- s018 -->
It was longer than mine by the breadth of two fingers, and wider, and the toes had spread in a way mine never have because I have worn shoes since I was three.

<!-- s019 -->
I lay there in the sand with my leg out and I understood that the story was not true.

<!-- s020 -->
![It was longer than mine by the breadth of two fingers.](media/ch10-b.jpg)

<!-- s021 -->
Here is the thing I have never been able to explain to anybody who has not lived alone.

<!-- s022 -->
For fifteen years I had wanted a sail. Every single day. I had climbed the hill and looked, in all weathers, for fifteen years, at an empty sea.

<!-- s023 -->
And the first proof I ever had that another human being existed within a thousand miles of me — one footprint, no threat, no violence, nothing but the plain fact of another man — sent me into a hole in the ground for three days.

<!-- s024 -->
I have thought about it for the rest of my life and this is the best I can do.

<!-- s025 -->
I did not want people. I wanted rescue.

<!-- s026 -->
Rescue is a thing that happens to you on your own terms, at your own convenience, on a ship with a captain who feeds you. What I had found on that beach was not rescue. It was somebody else's will, loose on my island, doing what it liked.

<!-- s027 -->
I had been the only fact in that place for fifteen years. Now I was not. And it turned out that a great deal of what I had been calling contentment had been resting quietly on being alone in charge.

<!-- s028 -->
That was not a comfortable thing to learn at forty-three.

<!-- s029 -->
⁂

<!-- s030 -->
I became, over the next two years, a very strange animal.

<!-- s031 -->
I moved the goats inland and split the herd in three, so that no one place would show a man that there was a herd at all.

<!-- s032 -->
I stopped burning fires by day. I cooked at night, under the overhang, where the smoke broke up in the rock before it got out.

<!-- s033 -->
I planted my grain in five small patches instead of one field, in among the scrub, where they looked like nothing.

<!-- s034 -->
I made a second entrance to my house that came out in a thicket, and I kept a ladder there, and I never once used it for eleven years and never once stopped keeping it ready.

<!-- s035 -->
And I walked everywhere on stone and shingle. Two years of walking the long way round, over rock, so as not to put my own foot into sand.

<!-- s036 -->
Fifteen years I had lived openly on that island like a man in his own kitchen. After the print, I lived in it like a mouse.

<!-- s037 -->
![This was a place they came to, and went away from.](media/ch10-c.jpg)

<!-- s038 -->
I found the rest of it in the third year, at the far end, on a beach on the south-west side that I had been to perhaps twice in eighteen years because there is nothing there.

<!-- s039 -->
There was a place where fires had been made.

<!-- s040 -->
There were bones. Human ones. I am not going to describe them and I am not going to pretend I stood there and reasoned calmly about the customs of other nations, because I did not. I went about forty yards up the beach and was violently sick, and then I sat down on a rock with my back to it.

<!-- s041 -->
They came from somewhere else. That was the first thing I made myself work out, sitting on that rock. This was not a village. Nobody lived here. This was a place they came to, in boats, from another shore, and did what they did, and went away.

<!-- s042 -->
Which meant they would come again.

<!-- s043 -->
And it meant that for eighteen years, while I was building fences and being pleased about butter, boats had been landing at the other end of my island, and I had never known.
