<!-- s001 -->
**Chapter Five. The House and the Boat**

<!-- s002 -->
I learned Arabic the way a dog learns it.

<!-- s003 -->
Nobody sat me down. Nobody explained a single rule. Words came at me attached to things: to a hoe, to a bucket, to *bring that here* and *not like that* and *are you deaf*. I picked them up one at a time, badly, in the wrong order, with no idea which of them were polite.

<!-- s004 -->
For about four months I spoke like a rude child. Then one evening I said something in the courtyard and the woman who brought the bread laughed, and I understood *why* she was laughing, and that was the day I could speak.

<!-- s005 -->
The boy taught me most of it. His name was Xury.

<!-- s006 -->
![I was property for two years.](media/ch05-a.jpg)

<!-- s007 -->
He was perhaps thirteen and he had been in that house longer than I had, and he was quicker than me at everything except figures.

<!-- s008 -->
I have to be careful here, because of how these things get told. He was not my little servant. Nobody had given him to me. We were two pieces of the same man's property, and if the master had sold either of us the other would have had no more say in it than a chair has.

<!-- s009 -->
That is what we had in common, and it is a strange, strong thing to have in common.

<!-- s010 -->
He taught me the words. I taught him to write his name, in the dust of the courtyard, with a stick. He made me do it about forty times and then he could do it, and he sat looking at it for a while, and then he rubbed it out with his foot before anybody came.

<!-- s011 -->
I did not ask why. I knew why.

<!-- s012 -->
⁂

<!-- s013 -->
My master's business was the sea, and after the first year he found out I was useful on it.

<!-- s014 -->
He kept a boat, a good one, with a little cabin forward and a lateen sail. He went out in her to fish, which is what men of means did there on a fine morning, the way men here go out with a gun.

<!-- s015 -->
I was sent along to work her. Xury was sent along to do everything else.

<!-- s016 -->
And I found out something that surprised me very much, which is that I was good at it.

<!-- s017 -->
I had the wind, and the way the boat sat, and the shape of the swell. I do not know where any of it came from, unless it came from eighteen years of standing on a quay watching — which I had always thought was time wasted.

<!-- s018 -->
By the second summer my master would sit in the stern with his line and let me sail her. Once he said to a friend of his, in Arabic, thinking I did not have enough of it yet: *this one has the sea in his hands.*

<!-- s019 -->
I have never in my life been so pleased and so sick at the same time.

<!-- s020 -->
⁂

<!-- s021 -->
I should say something about the sound now, because two years is long enough for a sound to stop being strange.

<!-- s022 -->
It went on being five times a day. That does not change.

<!-- s023 -->
What changed was me.

<!-- s024 -->
By the second year I knew the words, though nobody had taught them to me either, and I knew what they meant, though I would have denied being interested. I knew that the first one before dawn was the hardest and that a man who came out for it in the winter dark was doing something difficult.

<!-- s025 -->
I saw my master do it in a wet garden when he had a fever, and be short with everybody afterwards, and do it again the next morning.

<!-- s026 -->
I want to be exact about my state of mind, because it would be very easy to make this tidier than it was.

<!-- s027 -->
I did not believe. I did not want to believe. I was a slave in that house and I was not going to be talked into loving the thing that held me.

<!-- s028 -->
But I had grown up in a country where a man's religion was a coat he put on for an hour on Sunday. Here was a thing that cut the day into pieces and would not let anybody alone. I could not make myself despise it.

<!-- s029 -->
I filed it away. I told myself I would think about it when I was free.

<!-- s030 -->
I did think about it when I was free. It took me eleven more years and an island.

<!-- s031 -->
![I taught him to write his name in the dust of the courtyard.](media/ch05-b.jpg)

<!-- s032 -->
The chance came in the third spring, and it came because my master was too comfortable.

<!-- s033 -->
He was to take out two guests, and he had us provision the boat the night before, and in the morning he did not come. He sent word that we were to go out and fish for the house instead. One of his men would come to keep an eye on the pair of us: a grown man of his household, who was no sailor at all.

<!-- s034 -->
So there were three of us in the boat and one of them could not swim.

<!-- s035 -->
I had two years of that coast in my head by then. I knew the current. I knew where the wind went in the afternoon. I knew that nobody would think anything was wrong until nightfall, and that nobody would come out looking in the dark.

<!-- s036 -->
I had put a great deal of bread aboard the night before, and a jar of water more than we needed. I had told Xury nothing. A boy of thirteen cannot keep a secret in his face for twelve hours.

<!-- s037 -->
⁂

<!-- s038 -->
I did it about two hours out, with the shore a low brown line.

<!-- s039 -->
I want to set down what I actually did, without making it braver or better than it was.

<!-- s040 -->
I looked at the distance first. I made myself count: the swell, the set of the current, how far a man could swim with the sun where it was. I have wondered since whether that counting was mercy or bookkeeping. Perhaps at eighteen there is no difference.

<!-- s041 -->
Then I took him round the waist and put him over the side.

<!-- s042 -->
He came up shouting. I told him, in his own language, which I had learned in his own house, that the shore was there and he could reach it easily. I told him I had no wish to hurt him. And I told him that if he tried to come back over the gunwale, I would not be able to promise him anything.

<!-- s043 -->
He swore at me. Then he turned and swam.

<!-- s044 -->
I watched him until I was sure. He was going strongly. The last I saw of him he was standing up in the shallows about a mile off, still shouting things I could hear only as a noise.

<!-- s045 -->
I have thought about him a great deal more than he has probably thought about me.

<!-- s046 -->
![This one has the sea in his hands.](media/ch05-c.jpg)

<!-- s047 -->
Xury had not moved the whole time.

<!-- s048 -->
When I came back to the tiller he looked at me and said, very carefully, "Where are we going?"

<!-- s049 -->
And I said, "South."

<!-- s050 -->
He said, "South is nothing. South is a hundred days of nothing."

<!-- s051 -->
"Then we go until we find something."

<!-- s052 -->
He was quiet for a while. Then he said the sentence I have carried longer than almost anything anybody has ever said to me. He said:

<!-- s053 -->
"If they take us, they will not sell me. Do you understand? They will not sell me. They will make an example."

<!-- s054 -->
I did understand. That was the terrible part. I had a country somewhere, and a name in a ledger in York, and if the worst came I was a runaway who could be sold again.

<!-- s055 -->
He had none of that. What he was risking and what I was risking were not the same size at all, and he knew it exactly, and he came anyway.

<!-- s056 -->
Then he took the water jar and stowed it properly under the thwart, where it would not go over if we had weather.

<!-- s057 -->
We ran south with the land on our left hand until the light went. Then we ran south by the star my father had shown me on a cold roof in York. It stood lower in that sky than it had ever stood at home.

<!-- s058 -->
I remember thinking that he would have liked to know it worked.
