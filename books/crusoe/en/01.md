<!-- s001 -->
**Chapter One. The Word I Gave**

<!-- s002 -->
My father sold rope.

<!-- s003 -->
Not the thin kind you tie a parcel with. Ship rope. Thick as my wrist, brown as bread crust, heavy with tar. Our storehouse in York smelled of it — tar and hemp and dust — and I hated that smell the way only a boy of eighteen can hate a smell.

<!-- s004 -->
Every morning he counted the coils. Every evening he counted them again.

<!-- s005 -->
"Robinson," he said once, without looking up, "a man who counts his rope will never drown."

<!-- s006 -->
I thought it was the stupidest thing I had ever heard.

<!-- s007 -->
I was wrong. I paid for being wrong on an island with no name, four thousand miles from anyone who knew mine. But that comes later.

<!-- s008 -->
First you should know this. My father asked me for one thing. I gave him my word. I broke it before the month was out.

<!-- s009 -->
![My father counted the coils every morning, and every evening he counted them again.](media/ch01-a.jpg)

<!-- s010 -->
Our name was not really Crusoe.

<!-- s011 -->
My father came out of Bremen as a young man, and the name he carried was Kreutznaer, and York could not say it. So York filed it down the way a river files down a stone, and by the time I was born we were Crusoe, and no one remembered otherwise.

<!-- s012 -->
I have thought about that a great deal since. A name is not as fixed a thing as a boy imagines. Put it in enough strange mouths and it will come back to you changed.

<!-- s013 -->
Mine came back changed twice.

<!-- s014 -->
⁂

<!-- s015 -->
York is not a sea town. But the Ouse runs down to Hull, and Hull opens on the whole grey world. Every rope my father sold went down that river to be tied to something that floated.

<!-- s016 -->
I used to walk to the staith and watch the barges load.

<!-- s017 -->
Twice a year he took me to Hull to see the chandlers, and those two days were the only days of my year. The forest of masts. The stink and shout of it. Men off ships from Danzig and Lisbon and places I could not have found on a map, all of them loud, all of them going somewhere.

<!-- s018 -->
My father knew that about me. That was the trouble.

<!-- s019 -->
⁂

<!-- s020 -->
I had two brothers once.

<!-- s021 -->
The elder was a soldier. He was killed at Dunkirk, in a trench, at twenty-three. We know the date of it because a lieutenant wrote to us.

<!-- s022 -->
The second went to sea.

<!-- s023 -->
He was seventeen when he went and we never heard one word after, not from a port, not from a shipmate, not ever. He simply stopped.

<!-- s024 -->
We have no grave for either of them. For one we have a date. For the other we do not even have that.

<!-- s025 -->
After that my father stopped speaking about the sea the way other men in York spoke about it. To them it was a road. It was a living. It was the ordinary way a thing gets from one place to another. For him it stopped being a road. It became a mouth.

<!-- s026 -->
⁂

<!-- s027 -->
He tried to give me two things that year. I took neither.

<!-- s028 -->
The first was the counting. Every coil, every morning, every evening, and the number set down in a ledger in his small square hand. I told him a boy could learn nothing from a rope but how to be bored.

<!-- s029 -->
The second he gave me on the roof of the storehouse.

<!-- s030 -->
It was late, and cold, and I wanted to be asleep. He made me stand facing out over the dark town and he pointed north, at the star that does not move.

<!-- s031 -->
"Find that," he said. "Then you can find everything else."

<!-- s032 -->
He showed me how to hold my hand up against the sky and measure. He showed me how the star stood higher in the north and lower in the south. A man who watched it carefully could tell how far he had come. He made me do it four nights running and write the figures down.

<!-- s033 -->
"You will not always be in a place that tells you where you are," he said. "One day you may have to know it without being told."

<!-- s034 -->
I said yes, father. I said it three times. I looked at the star. And I thought about a ship I had seen at Hull, with a green hull and a crew of Portuguese.

<!-- s035 -->
I remembered almost none of it.

<!-- s036 -->
Almost.

<!-- s037 -->
And I did not know then that I would one day use that star to find something other than my way home.

<!-- s038 -->
!["Find that," he said. "Then you can find everything else."](media/ch01-b.jpg)

<!-- s039 -->
The trouble came in the shape of a friend of mine, who had a face that could talk anyone into anything.

<!-- s040 -->
His father owned a share in a vessel going down to London on the first of the month, and she was short one man.

<!-- s041 -->
"Not one man," he said. "One boy. They want a boy who can write."

<!-- s042 -->
I laughed at him.

<!-- s043 -->
"They want a boy who can write and reckon and keep a cargo list. There is not one on the whole quay at Hull. Every boy on that quay was raised to haul and not to read."

<!-- s044 -->
And there it was. My father had made me a boy with ink on his fingers. I could count a hundred coils and set the number down in a straight line. That was exactly the thing that could buy me a place on a ship.

<!-- s045 -->
He had built the door himself. He just never thought I would find the handle.

<!-- s046 -->
⁂

<!-- s047 -->
He asked me for my word on a Thursday.

<!-- s048 -->
He had heard something. York is a small city for a secret. He did not shout at me, which I had been ready for. He put down the ledger, and he looked at me for a long time, and then he said:

<!-- s049 -->
"Give me your word that you will not go."

<!-- s050 -->
I could have said no. He would have shouted then, and I could have shouted back, and I could have walked out through a door I had kicked open honestly.

<!-- s051 -->
Instead I said, "I give you my word."

<!-- s052 -->
I have turned that moment over more times than I can count. I did not lie because I was afraid of him. I lied because it was easier, and because I had already decided, and because a promise seemed to me then like a thing made out of breath.

<!-- s053 -->
He nodded. He picked up the ledger again. And — this is the part I cannot get out of my head — he looked relieved.

<!-- s054 -->
⁂

<!-- s055 -->
I went down to Hull on the last day of August.

<!-- s056 -->
I told my mother I was going about a debt at the tannery. She gave me bread and cheese for the walk. She told me to give her greeting to a man named Wilkin, whom I had no intention of greeting. And she said what she always said, which was that I should be back before dark.

<!-- s057 -->
I took my own savings, which were small and honestly mine.

<!-- s058 -->
And I took one thing that was not.

<!-- s059 -->
From the back of the storehouse, where the short lengths lay that were too small to sell, I took a coil of my father's rope. Twenty feet of it. Good hemp, well laid, tarred the winter before.

<!-- s060 -->
I have never been able to explain why. I did not need it. I have thought since that I wanted to carry something of his out through that door. I would not carry the two things he had actually tried to give me.

<!-- s061 -->
It is the only thing I ever stole in my life.

<!-- s062 -->
It is also the reason I am alive to write this down.

<!-- s063 -->
![There was a man standing at the end of the pier.](media/ch01-c.jpg)

<!-- s064 -->
We went out on the first of September, in the year 1651, on the morning tide.

<!-- s065 -->
I stood at the rail and watched Hull turn from a town into a shape.

<!-- s066 -->
The steeples went first, then the roofs, then everything but the long grey line of the pier with the surf breaking white along it.

<!-- s067 -->
There was a man standing at the end of the pier.

<!-- s068 -->
He was too far off. He was a stroke of dark against the grey, no taller than my thumbnail. I could not have sworn to his face in front of any judge in the world. There are fishermen out there every morning of the year.

<!-- s069 -->
But he did not move, and he did not turn, and he was still standing there when the shape of him went into the haze.

<!-- s070 -->
I did not raise my hand.

<!-- s071 -->
I have had a great many years to think about that, and I still cannot tell you why.
