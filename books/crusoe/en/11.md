<!-- s001 -->
**Chapter Eleven. Friday**

<!-- s002 -->
They came three more times in six years, and I watched all three from the hill with my heart going, and I did nothing.

<!-- s003 -->
I had worked it out on paper, the way I work everything out. I had four guns and one pair of hands. I could kill perhaps five of them before they reached me, and there were never fewer than twenty.

<!-- s004 -->
But that was not really why.

<!-- s005 -->
The real reason took me two years to say plainly to myself, and it was this: they had never once done me any harm, and had never once come looking for me, and I was not the magistrate of that ocean.

<!-- s006 -->
I hated what they did on that beach. I hate it now. But I noticed, sitting on my hill with a gun across my knees, how badly I wanted a reason to go down there — and how much of that wanting was eighteen years of having nobody to be angry at.

<!-- s007 -->
A man alone becomes very good at dressing up what he wants as what is right.

<!-- s008 -->
![I do not remember standing up.](media/ch11-a.jpg)

<!-- s009 -->
The twenty-fourth year. March, by my post.

<!-- s010 -->
There were two boats and about thirty men, and they had three prisoners with them, and I was up on the hill lying flat in the grass with the glass.

<!-- s011 -->
They killed one almost at once.

<!-- s012 -->
And the second one ran.

<!-- s013 -->
⁂

<!-- s014 -->
He came straight up the beach and into the trees on my side of the island, running the way a man runs when running is the last thing he has, and three of them came after him.

<!-- s015 -->
He was faster than they were. He was much faster. He had about four hundred yards on them by the time he reached the creek, and he went into it and across without slowing and came up the near bank into the scrub, and that is where I was.

<!-- s016 -->
I had perhaps ten seconds to decide.

<!-- s017 -->
I would like to tell you that I weighed it. I did not weigh anything. I came out of the scrub between him and the three of them with a gun in each hand and I do not remember standing up.

<!-- s018 -->
I killed one. The second I hit with the barrel of the empty gun. The third stopped dead about fifteen paces off, looked at me for one long moment, and turned and ran back the way he had come — and he must have told them something, because the boats went before dark and did not come back to that island for eleven years.

<!-- s019 -->
⁂

<!-- s020 -->
The man I had pulled out of it was on the ground behind me, and when I turned round he did something I have never been able to get out of my head.

<!-- s021 -->
He came up on his knees, and took my foot, and put it on his own head.

<!-- s022 -->
I knew exactly what it meant. Anybody would.

<!-- s023 -->
And I want to write down honestly what happened inside me in the two or three seconds while my foot was on that man's head, because if I leave it out the rest is worthless.

<!-- s024 -->
For about two seconds, I liked it.

<!-- s025 -->
Twenty-four years alone. Not one voice. And here was a living man on his knees telling me, in the oldest language there is, that I was the master and he was the thing.

<!-- s026 -->
Then I remembered a hot garden in Salé, and a boy of thirteen writing his own name in the dust with a stick and rubbing it out with his foot before anybody came.

<!-- s027 -->
I took my foot off his head so fast I nearly fell over.

<!-- s028 -->
⁂

<!-- s029 -->
I got him up. He did not understand why. He tried twice more over the next two days to do it again and I stopped him both times, and the second time I was angry, and he could not think what he had done wrong.

<!-- s030 -->
That took months to undo. Longer than teaching him English.

<!-- s031 -->
I have thought since that the cruellest thing about what had been done to that man, and to me, and to Xury, is how quickly it teaches the person it is done to that it is normal.

<!-- s032 -->
![I took my foot off his head so fast I nearly fell over.](media/ch11-b.jpg)

<!-- s033 -->
He was, I would guess, about twenty-six. He had a healed break in his left forearm that had set crooked. He could run further than any man I have ever met.

<!-- s034 -->
And within a month it was perfectly clear which of us was the ignorant one.

<!-- s035 -->
I had been eating a small blue crab for years that he would not let me touch after the rains. I had never worked out why the stream by the north valley ran low in some dry seasons and not others; he found the seep in one afternoon by looking at where two kinds of grass met. He could tell me at dawn whether it would be worth going out in the boat, from the birds, and he was right about four times in five, and I was right about half the time after twenty-four years.

<!-- s036 -->
He made me a fishing line in an evening that was better than anything I had made in two decades.

<!-- s037 -->
I taught him English, and to read it. I taught him figures. And on a clear night I took him up the hill and showed him the star that does not move, and how to hold a hand up to it, and how the whole of the sky turns round that one point and that point alone.

<!-- s038 -->
He liked that better than anything else I ever showed him.

<!-- s039 -->
⁂

<!-- s040 -->
I called him Friday.

<!-- s041 -->
I did it on the first day, because it was a Friday by my post, and because I had to call him something, and because — let me be exact — it did not occur to me for one second to ask.

<!-- s042 -->
I asked him in the fifth month.

<!-- s043 -->
He told me. It was three sounds and I got the first one wrong twice and the second one wrong three times, and he laughed at me, which was the first time I ever heard him laugh, and he said it slowly four times until I had it.

<!-- s044 -->
And I stood there with his name finally in my mouth and thought about Bremen.

<!-- s045 -->
My father's name went into English mouths as Kreutznaer and came out of them, worn down, as Crusoe. My own name went into a house in Salé and came out two syllables shorter, and I lay on a mat and thought bitterly about what had been taken.

<!-- s046 -->
And then I had done exactly the same thing to another man inside about ninety seconds of meeting him, and had not noticed, and would never have noticed if I had not thought to ask.

<!-- s047 -->
I have set him down in these pages as Friday all the same.

<!-- s048 -->
I thought about it for a long time before I decided. But this is meant to be a true account, and in a true account a man's mistakes have to be left where he made them, or nobody learns anything, least of all him.

<!-- s049 -->
⁂

<!-- s050 -->
He knew about the grave.

<!-- s051 -->
I took him up the valley in the second year, to the shelf of grass, and he went quiet when he saw the stones and would not walk on that side of the stream.

<!-- s052 -->
Then he told me, in the broken English we had between us then, that his people knew this island.

<!-- s053 -->
They did not come to it. That was the point — the men in the boats came from further along the coast and his people thought them fools. But there was an old story, from before his grandfather, about a man who came to this island out of the sea in a broken boat.

<!-- s054 -->
The story had two details in it and both of them went through me like cold water.

<!-- s055 -->
The man had lived here a long time, alone. And he had gone out every morning before the sun and stood and spoken, and bowed, facing *away* from the place the sun came up — which is exactly what a story would remember, because on that coast it makes no sense at all.

<!-- s056 -->
Then he died. And there was another man with him at the end, who put the stones on him and cut the writing, and afterwards went away north in a canoe by himself, and was never heard of again.

<!-- s057 -->
⁂

<!-- s058 -->
Friday asked me, that autumn, about the two posts outside my door.

<!-- s059 -->
I told him what they were for, and how I had worked out the line with a dead man's brass and nine noons and a star.

<!-- s060 -->
He thought about it, and then he asked the question I have never entirely answered.

<!-- s061 -->
He said: "You face a place you have never seen."

<!-- s062 -->
I said yes.

<!-- s063 -->
He said, "How do you know it is there?"

<!-- s064 -->
And I said — because it was true, and because it was all I had — "The same way I knew England was there, in the sixteenth year. I never saw it either. But I came from somewhere, and so did the man under those stones, and he faced the same way, and he had never seen it either, and neither had whoever taught him."

<!-- s065 -->
Friday considered that for about a week.

<!-- s066 -->
Then he asked me to show him how the brass disc worked, and I did, and he was better at reading the scale than I was within a month, because his eyes were twenty years younger.

<!-- s067 -->
That is as much as I am going to say about it. Anything more would be me tidying up eleven years into a neat shape, and it was not neat.

<!-- s068 -->
![He liked that better than anything else I ever showed him.](media/ch11-c.jpg)

<!-- s069 -->
We were together eleven years.

<!-- s070 -->
I have skipped almost all of it and I am sorry for that, because they were the best years I had, and there is very little to tell. We made a bigger boat and got it into the water, which had beaten me alone for two decades. We put a proper roof on the house. We had a bad year with the goats and a good one with the grain.

<!-- s071 -->
He learned to read well enough to read my paper, and did, and told me that the part about the footprint was too long, which it is.

<!-- s072 -->
And on an ordinary morning in the thirty-fifth year we went up the hill because it was clear, the way I had gone up it every clear morning for thirty-five years, and there was a ship in the offing.

<!-- s073 -->
An English ship, at anchor, about two miles out.

<!-- s074 -->
And a boat already in the water, pulling for my shore.
