**Chapter Six. What I Would Not Sell**

<!-- s002 -->
Freedom, for the first eleven days, was mostly about water.

<!-- s003 -->
We went south with the land on our left hand and we did not dare go ashore. For the first week we were still in country where a boat like ours would be known, and a boy like Xury would be worth money to whoever brought him back.

<!-- s004 -->
So we drank a little and we were thirsty all day and we drank a little more.

<!-- s005 -->
I had done a calculation on the second morning and I did not tell him the answer.

<!-- s006 -->
That is one of the small ugly parts of being the older one. I knew we had nine days of water at what we were drinking. I knew that nine days south would put us nowhere in particular. And I sat at the tiller with that in my head and said pleasant things about the weather.

<!-- s007 -->
![We lay in the bottom of the boat with the sail over us.](media/ch06-a.jpg)

<!-- s008 -->
We went ashore on the twelfth day because we had to.

<!-- s009 -->
I ran her into a creek mouth at dusk, and we sat in the boat in the dark with the jar empty and listened to the country.

<!-- s010 -->
It was not quiet. That is what nobody tells you. There were things calling all night in three or four different voices, and once something big went into the water somewhere upstream, and the noise it made going in was not the noise a small thing makes.

<!-- s011 -->
Xury did not sleep. Neither did I. We lay in the bottom of the boat with the sail over us like two children hiding from a thunderstorm, which is exactly what we were.

<!-- s012 -->
At first light we found the stream, and it was sweet, and we filled everything we had.

<!-- s013 -->
And while we were filling the last jar, the thing on the bank stood up.

<!-- s014 -->
⁂

<!-- s015 -->
I had the master's gun. I had loaded it perhaps six times in my life.

<!-- s016 -->
It was a big cat. I never learned what kind and I have deliberately never looked it up, because in my memory it is the size of a pony and I would rather keep it that way.

<!-- s017 -->
It was about forty feet off, up on the bank above us, and it was not crouching or snarling or any of the things that would have been useful. It was standing quite still with its head down, working out whether we were worth the walk.

<!-- s018 -->
Xury said, very quietly, "Do not run."

<!-- s019 -->
I want that recorded. I was twenty-one years old and had crossed an ocean. The piece of advice that saved my life on that riverbank came from a thirteen-year-old. He had never been more than a mile from Salé until eleven days before.

<!-- s020 -->
I did not run. I put the gun up and I waited until it took two steps, and then I fired.

<!-- s021 -->
I hit it somewhere that mattered. It went down and got up and went sideways into the bushes, and we heard it going away up the slope for a long time, and then we heard it stop.

<!-- s022 -->
We found it an hour later, dead, about two hundred yards off. I have no pride at all in the shot. I have some in the fact that neither of us ran.

<!-- s023 -->
⁂

<!-- s024 -->
People came out of the trees that afternoon to look at us.

<!-- s025 -->
There were perhaps a dozen, and they stood off at a distance and looked, and we stood by the boat and looked back, and this went on for a long time.

<!-- s026 -->
Then Xury did the sensible thing, which I would not have thought of.

<!-- s027 -->
He walked ten steps away from the boat and put down the gun and the knife where they could see him do it. Then he walked back, pointed up the slope towards the dead animal, and made a wide gesture: *it is there, and it is yours.*

<!-- s028 -->
They understood. Of course they understood. They had been watching us since dawn and they knew perfectly well what had happened, and I think they had been waiting to see whether we would be stupid about it.

<!-- s029 -->
They took the skin and most of the meat. In the evening two of them came down with a clay pot of something cooked and a bundle of dried fish, and stood while we ate some of it. I understood later that this was courtesy, and also good sense.

<!-- s030 -->
They were not simple people. They were people with a river, a village, and no particular reason to trust two strangers with a gun.

<!-- s031 -->
We stayed three days. I have often thought that if the ship had not come we might have stayed for good, and that it would not have been the worst life.

<!-- s032 -->
![It was standing quite still, working out whether we were worth the walk.](media/ch06-b.jpg)

<!-- s033 -->
The Portuguese ship came up the coast on the fourth morning.

<!-- s034 -->
I put out and stood across her bows with the sail flat and my shirt on an oar. She backed her topsail and let us come alongside. A rope came down, and that was the end of the running.

<!-- s035 -->
The captain was a small grey man from Lisbon who had been forty years at sea. He fed us. He would not take the boat as payment, though it was worth something and I offered it twice.

<!-- s036 -->
He said a thing I have repeated to a great many people since. He said that he had once been picked up out of the water himself, and that the man who did it took nothing. He had been paying that debt to strangers for thirty years, and expected to die still owing on it.

<!-- s037 -->
Then, on the fourth evening out, over the remains of his dinner, he made me an offer for Xury.

<!-- s038 -->
⁂

<!-- s039 -->
He did it kindly. That is important. He was not a slaver and there was nothing in his face but ordinary business.

<!-- s040 -->
He said that the boy was quick, that he would train him, and he named a sum — a good sum, a sum that would have set me up in Brazil with land instead of debt.

<!-- s041 -->
And he added, because he was a decent man by the standards of his trade, that he would set him free in ten years.

<!-- s042 -->
I said no.

<!-- s043 -->
He was surprised. He asked me why, and I found that I could not answer him for a moment, and then I said:

<!-- s044 -->
"Because eleven days ago I was worth about the same, and somebody was making that arithmetic about me."

<!-- s045 -->
He looked at me for a while.

<!-- s046 -->
Then he said, "You will be poor in Brazil."

<!-- s047 -->
I said I knew.

<!-- s048 -->
And he said, "Well," and poured more wine, and never raised it again. In the morning he offered Xury a place aboard as a paid boy, at a real wage. He did not have to do that. I have thought about it every time anyone has told me the world is simply cruel.

<!-- s049 -->
⁂

<!-- s050 -->
Xury took it. He was right to take it.

<!-- s051 -->
He stayed with that ship four years and I did not see him again for a long time. When I did, he was a grown man with a mate's certificate and a scar on his hand. He had a great deal to say about my seamanship, none of it flattering.

<!-- s052 -->
But that is later, and it belongs to another part of this.

<!-- s053 -->
!["Because eleven days ago I was worth about the same."](media/ch06-c.jpg)

<!-- s054 -->
We came into Brazil in the spring, and I went up the country with almost nothing.

<!-- s055 -->
Here is the thing I want to be honest about, because it is the whole point of everything that comes after, and if I dress it up the rest will make no sense.

<!-- s056 -->
I was proud of myself.

<!-- s057 -->
I had refused good money for a human being, and I knew that most men in that harbour would not have refused it, and I carried that around inside me like a warm coal for years.

<!-- s058 -->
I was so pleased about the large thing I had got right that I never once looked at the small thing I had got wrong.

<!-- s059 -->
My father had asked me for one sentence. I had given him my word, and broken it, and let him stand on the end of a pier and watch a ship take his last son away.

<!-- s060 -->
I had crossed an ocean and refused a fortune and I had never written him a letter.

<!-- s061 -->
Not one. In four years.

<!-- s062 -->
There is a kind of man who will do the noble thing in front of witnesses and cannot do the plain thing in private. I was twenty-two years old, and I was exactly that man. It was going to cost me twenty-eight years of my life.
