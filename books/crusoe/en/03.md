<!-- s001 -->
**Chapter Three. The Hold**

<!-- s002 -->
London took my money in five weeks and gave me nothing back.

<!-- s003 -->
I copied letters for a scrivener near the Fleet. It is the only trade my father's ledgers ever got me. I slept in a room with four other men and a chimney that did not draw. In the mornings I went down to the pool and looked at the ships the way a hungry man looks in at a cookshop window.

<!-- s004 -->
I tried to sign aboard three of them.

<!-- s005 -->
The first master asked for my indenture papers. The second asked who I had sailed with. I said one voyage out of Hull that ended on the sand at Yarmouth. He laughed so hard he had to put his cup down.

<!-- s006 -->
The third did not ask anything. He looked at my hands, which were soft, and at my coat, which was too good. Then he said what they all meant: *you are somebody's runaway son, and I am not taking that trouble aboard my ship.*

<!-- s007 -->
He was right, of course.

<!-- s008 -->
Being right about a boy has never once stopped him.

<!-- s009 -->
![A space the shape of a coffin, and no way out of it.](media/ch03-a.jpg)

<!-- s010 -->
She was loading for the Guinea coast and she was to drop down river on the Tuesday tide.

<!-- s011 -->
I went aboard on the Monday night with a sack over my shoulder, up the plank behind two porters. Nobody looked at me twice. I was carrying something, and moving like a man who knew where he was going. I have since learned that this will get a person into almost anywhere.

<!-- s012 -->
I went down through the main hatch while they were still striking cargo below. I got in behind the water casks on the starboard side. There was a gap there between the casks and the ship's timbers, about as wide as my shoulders and about as long as I was tall.

<!-- s013 -->
I had bread, a bottle, a knife, a horn of tinder I could not use in a hold full of tar, and twenty feet of my father's rope.

<!-- s014 -->
My plan was simple, and I want to set it down honestly, because it was the plan of an idiot.

<!-- s015 -->
I would lie hidden two days. Then I would come up. We would be at sea, they could not put me ashore, and a ship is always short of hands. They would swear at me and put me to work and by Guinea I would be a sailor.

<!-- s016 -->
Every part of that was true except the timing, and the timing was the whole thing.

<!-- s017 -->
⁂

<!-- s018 -->
They battened the hatches on the Tuesday, in the rain, and I lay in the dark and listened to the ship come alive over my head.

<!-- s019 -->
Then the sea got up.

<!-- s020 -->
I do not know how big a blow it was. From where I lay, all weather is the same weather. What I know is that on the second night the cargo began to work.

<!-- s021 -->
That is the word the sailors use, and it is a mild word for what it is. A ship's cargo is stowed tight. When it is not tight enough, the roll starts to move it, a finger's width at a time. And every roll makes the next roll easier.

<!-- s022 -->
I heard it start. A grinding, somewhere forward, that had not been there before.

<!-- s023 -->
Then something came down.

<!-- s024 -->
I do not know what it was, even now. Bales, I think, and a tier of small casks on top of them. It came down across the mouth of my gap with a noise that went through the whole ship. When it stopped, the space I was lying in had no way out of it.

<!-- s025 -->
I put my hands out and felt along the whole of it, twice, on my knees in the black.

<!-- s026 -->
Timbers on one side. Casks on the other. A wall of something wedged and immovable at the end where I had come in.

<!-- s027 -->
And at the other end, a hole about the size of my chest, going away into the cargo.

<!-- s028 -->
⁂

<!-- s029 -->
I want to say that I was brave.

<!-- s030 -->
What I actually did was scream until my voice broke. Then I beat on the casks with the handle of my knife until my hand would not close. Then I lay down in a space the shape of a coffin and shook for what I am fairly sure was several hours.

<!-- s031 -->
Nobody came. Nobody could have come. There was a foot of oak and thirty tons of cargo between me and the men walking about over my head, and above all that there was a gale.

<!-- s032 -->
When I stopped, I was very calm, and the calm frightened me more than the screaming had.

<!-- s033 -->
Because in the calm I understood the arithmetic. Nobody was going to open that hatch until fine weather. Fine weather might be a week. My bottle held perhaps a pint.

<!-- s034 -->
⁂

<!-- s035 -->
Here is the thing I have never been able to explain to anyone who has not been in the dark.

<!-- s036 -->
It is not the dark that undoes you. It is that time stops.

<!-- s037 -->
You cannot tell an hour from four hours. You sleep and wake and have no idea which side of the sleep you are on. Twice I was certain that days had passed, and twice I later worked out that it had been a single night.

<!-- s038 -->
A man in that state stops being able to think, and then he stops being able to want to think, and after that he is finished, whatever water he has.

<!-- s039 -->
What saved me was the bell.

<!-- s040 -->
I heard it on the third or fourth time of waking: a small far sound coming down through the deck, and then, some while after, the same sound again.

<!-- s041 -->
A ship strikes her bell every half hour. Eight bells, and the watch changes, and it begins again at one.

<!-- s042 -->
I lay in the dark and counted bells.

<!-- s043 -->
I counted them for the rest of the time I was down there. When I slept I lost the count, and had to pick it up again from the next watch change. I marked the days by cutting the wood of the cask beside my head with the point of my knife.

<!-- s044 -->
My father counted his rope twice a day for thirty years. I had told him a boy could learn nothing from that but how to be bored.

<!-- s045 -->
In the hold of that ship I learned what he had actually been teaching. A man who has a number can hold on to it. A man with nothing to count comes apart.

<!-- s046 -->
![I paid the rope out behind me as I went.](media/ch03-b.jpg)

<!-- s047 -->
The water was the second problem and it was worse than the first.

<!-- s048 -->
I found it by knocking. A full cask and an empty cask do not answer the same. After some hours of crawling and knocking with my knuckles I had found four that answered full. They were all of them out beyond the hole at the end of my space.

<!-- s049 -->
Getting to them was where the rope came in.

<!-- s050 -->
I could not see. I could not leave a mark. A man who crawls twenty feet into a stack of cargo in absolute darkness and then turns around has no way on earth of knowing which turning brought him in.

<!-- s051 -->
So I made one end fast to the cask by my head, and paid the rope out behind me as I went. When I wanted to come back, I came back along it hand over hand.

<!-- s052 -->
Twenty feet of good hemp, well laid, tarred the winter before.

<!-- s053 -->
I had taken it out of my father's storehouse because I wanted to steal something and it was the thing nearest the door. That is the whole of the reason. There was no sense in it.

<!-- s054 -->
I have thought a great deal since about how little of my life I have arranged, and how much of it has been arranged for me.

<!-- s055 -->
⁂

<!-- s056 -->
The casks were oak and the bungs were driven home hard and set in the head, where I could not get at them.

<!-- s057 -->
So I made a hole.

<!-- s058 -->
I put the point of the knife into a stave, low down, and I turned it, and I kept turning it. It took — I do not know. Two watches. Three. The blade went in about a finger's depth and then would not go further. I sat back and cried a little. Then I went at it again from a different angle, and broke the plug of wood out sideways.

<!-- s059 -->
The water came out over my hand.

<!-- s060 -->
I have drunk better water since and I have never once enjoyed it as much.

<!-- s061 -->
Then I did the sensible thing, which surprised me: I whittled a peg and drove it into the hole. Because a cask that runs out into the bilge is worth nothing to anybody, and I had understood by then that I might be down there a long time.

<!-- s062 -->
There was salt beef in that hold too. I found a broached cask of it on the second crawl, and I lay with my hand on the meat and made myself put it back.

<!-- s063 -->
Salt meat and a pint of water a day is a way of dying that takes longer and hurts more.

<!-- s064 -->
I lived on ship's bread, which was in a cask I could reach and which I have loathed ever since.

<!-- s065 -->
⁂

<!-- s066 -->
They opened the hatch on the eleventh day.

<!-- s067 -->
I knew it was the eleventh because I had eleven marks on the cask beside my head. I have been proud of very few things in my life the way I am proud of those eleven marks.

<!-- s068 -->
Light came down through the cargo in a grey bar full of dust and it was the most violent thing I had ever seen. I had to put my arm over my eyes.

<!-- s069 -->
Then I shouted, and a man's voice above said, very slowly, "There is something down there."

<!-- s070 -->
![They opened the hatch on the eleventh day.](media/ch03-c.jpg)

<!-- s071 -->
The master was not kind, and I have never held it against him.

<!-- s072 -->
A stowaway is not a bold lad in a story. He is a man stealing a passage, eating stores that were counted out for a crew. And the ship will hang for it if she is short of water in the doldrums.

<!-- s073 -->
He put me on the pumps, and on the worst of the beef. He told me I would have no wage. And he told me that when we made a port, he would put me ashore in it, whatever port it was.

<!-- s074 -->
Then, that evening, he sent for me again.

<!-- s075 -->
He asked how I had got water.

<!-- s076 -->
I told him about the knocking, and the knife, and the peg.

<!-- s077 -->
He looked at me for a long moment and said, "Who taught you to plug it after?"

<!-- s078 -->
And I said, "Nobody. My father sold rope."

<!-- s079 -->
He did not understand that, and I did not explain it.

<!-- s080 -->
I went down into the dark of that hold a boy who thought his father was a frightened old man with a wall built out of bad luck. I came up out of it with eleven knife marks, a peg in a water cask, and twenty feet of stolen rope coiled on my arm.

<!-- s081 -->
I have never since called either of his two dull gifts dull.
