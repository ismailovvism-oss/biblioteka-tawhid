**Chapter Two. The Storm**

<!-- s002 -->
I was sick before we cleared the river.

<!-- s003 -->
Nobody told me it would be like that. In my head I had been standing at the bow with the wind in my face, going somewhere. What actually happened was that I lay in the scuppers with my mouth open. I brought up bread and cheese until there was nothing left to bring up. Then I went on bringing up nothing.

<!-- s004 -->
I thought I was dying. I remember being almost glad about it.

<!-- s005 -->
What I could not forgive was that nobody cared.

<!-- s006 -->
Men stepped over me. One of them, going past with a coil on his shoulder, said "First time," in the voice you would use to say that it looked like rain. That was the whole of the sympathy I got in eleven hours.

<!-- s007 -->
I understand it now. Every man on that deck had lain where I was lying, once. To them I was not suffering. I was arriving.

<!-- s008 -->
![By evening the sky and the water were the same colour.](media/ch02-a.jpg)

<!-- s009 -->
By the second morning I was well, and by the second noon I was insufferable.

<!-- s010 -->
The wind was fair, the sea was long and easy, and the ship went over it the way a hand goes over a table. I stood at the rail with salt drying on my face and I thought: this is it, then. This is the thing my father was so frightened of.

<!-- s011 -->
I decided he was an old man who had been unlucky twice and had built a wall out of it.

<!-- s012 -->
I want to be fair to myself. I was eighteen. Nobody is sensible at eighteen.

<!-- s013 -->
But I want to be fair to him too. So I will say plainly what I thought, standing there, with the coast of my own country going by on the left hand. I thought I was better than him.

<!-- s014 -->
⁂

<!-- s015 -->
The wind began to rise on the fourth day, and it did not stop rising.

<!-- s016 -->
That is the part I cannot get people to understand. A storm at sea is not a thing that arrives. It is a thing that keeps arriving. Every hour it is worse than the hour before. Every hour you tell yourself that surely now it has done its worst. And every hour the sea shows you that you have no idea what its worst is.

<!-- s017 -->
By evening the sky and the water were the same colour and there was no line between them.

<!-- s018 -->
We ran in behind Yarmouth and let go both anchors with the rest of the shipping. There were a dozen sail in the Roads, all of us riding it out, all of us pitching. I could see the men on the nearest ship standing at their rail. They were looking at us exactly the way I was looking at them.

<!-- s019 -->
The mate came past me and I asked him if it was bad.

<!-- s020 -->
He said, "It's blowing."

<!-- s021 -->
I have never in my life wanted so badly for a man to lie to me.

<!-- s022 -->
⁂

<!-- s023 -->
I made my vow at about two in the morning.

<!-- s024 -->
I had my arms around a stanchion and my face against the wood and the whole world was tipping. Green water came over the waist and went out through the ports. Somewhere below me something heavy had got loose and was going from side to side with a noise like a door being knocked down, over and over.

<!-- s025 -->
And I said — out loud, though nobody could have heard me — that if I were let live, I would go home.

<!-- s026 -->
Not next year. Not after one more voyage.

<!-- s027 -->
Home, on my own two feet, up the street to the storehouse door. I would say what I had done. And I would stand there while it was said back to me.

<!-- s028 -->
I meant it. That is the thing. People suppose a man in a storm does not mean it. I have never meant anything more.

<!-- s029 -->
![They cut away the foremast to ease her.](media/ch02-b.jpg)

<!-- s030 -->
At first light the cable parted.

<!-- s031 -->
I did not know what had happened. I only knew that the motion changed, and that men began running who had not been running before. The master came out of the roundhouse with his hair flat to his head. He started giving orders in a voice like a hammer.

<!-- s032 -->
We were dragging. Then we were dragging faster.

<!-- s033 -->
They cut away the foremast to ease her. I watched a man go up with an axe in weather that should have taken him off the ship and I thought, quite clearly: I could not do that. Not for any money. Not to save my own life.

<!-- s034 -->
She came upright a little when the mast went. It bought us maybe an hour.

<!-- s035 -->
Then the carpenter came up out of the hold and did not say anything to anybody, only went straight to the master, and after that they fired the gun.

<!-- s036 -->
I did not know what the gun meant either. I learned. It means: we are going down, and any boat that can reach us should come.

<!-- s037 -->
⁂

<!-- s038 -->
A boat came.

<!-- s039 -->
I will not pretend I remember it well. I remember the rope in my hands, and that the rope was wet. I remember that the drop from our rail to their thwart kept changing by about six feet. Up and down, up and down. And I had to choose the moment myself, because nobody had time to choose it for me.

<!-- s040 -->
I remember thinking, with a sort of cold surprise: my father would know exactly what kind of rope this is.

<!-- s041 -->
Then I was in the boat with a man's boot in my back and somebody was shouting at me to get down and stay down.

<!-- s042 -->
Eleven of us got off. Four did not.

<!-- s043 -->
We were maybe two hundred yards away when she went. It was not a great crash and roar the way it is in stories. She simply got lower, and then lower, and then the sea closed over the place where she had been. It went on doing what it had been doing all night, as though nothing had been taken out of the middle of it at all.

<!-- s044 -->
That is what frightened me most. Not the noise. The lack of it.

<!-- s045 -->
⁂

<!-- s046 -->
They put us ashore at Yarmouth and the town was kind to us, which happens more often than people expect.

<!-- s047 -->
The master found me on the second day. He was not angry. That was worse than if he had been. He asked me whose son I was and why I had come. I told him, and he listened. Then he said something I have carried for the rest of my life.

<!-- s048 -->
He said: "Young man, you ought to take this for a plain sign that you are not to be a seafaring man."

<!-- s049 -->
And then, because he was honest, he added: "Though I have said that to four lads and three of them are at sea now."

<!-- s050 -->
I laughed. He did not.

<!-- s051 -->
![She simply got lower, and then lower.](media/ch02-c.jpg)

<!-- s052 -->
York is four days' walk from Yarmouth. I had money enough for the road.

<!-- s053 -->
I have crossed oceans since. I have walked in country where the ground was on fire under a thin crust, and I have gone into the sea at night knowing what was in it. I want that understood before I say the next thing.

<!-- s054 -->
Four days' walk was the only journey I have ever found impossible.

<!-- s055 -->
I got as far as the road out of the town. I stood there with the milestone in front of me, and I could see the whole of it. The street. The door. The smell of tar coming out to meet me. And my father's face, doing the arithmetic of what my being there meant.

<!-- s056 -->
He would have taken me in. I knew that then and I know it now. He would not even have shouted.

<!-- s057 -->
That was exactly the trouble. I could have borne shouting.

<!-- s058 -->
I turned south instead, towards London, and I told myself it was only until I had something to show him.

<!-- s059 -->
I have noticed since that a man will cross an ocean to avoid a conversation.
